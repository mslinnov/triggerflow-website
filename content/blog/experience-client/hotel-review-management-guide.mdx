## Why do hotel reviews matter so much?

Reviews directly influence booking decisions, search ranking, and pricing power — making them one of the highest-leverage levers in any hotel's commercial strategy.

| What reviews affect | How |
|---|---|
| **Booking conversion** | Travelers compare scores and read recent reviews before confirming; a higher, fresher score converts more browsers into bookers |
| **Google local ranking** | Volume, recency, and response rate are signals in Google's local algorithm — more actively managed reviews means better map pack position |
| **Pricing power** | A score visibly higher than your comp-set supports an ADR premium while still converting, protecting both occupancy and margin |

Three mechanisms drive this:

**Conversion.** Travelers consistently consult reviews before booking. A property with a strong, recent score and active responses converts meaningfully better than a competitor at a similar price point — even when the competitor's product is comparable.

**Visibility.** Google's local algorithm favors actively managed properties. Review volume, recency, and the fact that the hotel responds all contribute to ranking in the map pack. More visibility means more direct traffic, fewer OTA bookings, and less commission paid.

**Pricing.** A reputation clearly stronger than comparable properties gives you the confidence to hold rate. When your score is visibly better, guests are less likely to push back on a price difference — and rate integrity compounds over time.

The reverse is equally real: a cluster of unanswered negative reviews, or a score that has slipped below the comp-set, costs bookings silently and continuously.

## Which review platforms should hotels prioritize?

**Google is the non-negotiable priority for most hotels**, followed by Booking.com for distribution-heavy properties and TripAdvisor for markets where it still drives significant booking intent.

<ImageBlock id="platforms" />

| Platform | Why it matters | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| **Google** | Powers local search, Google Maps, and Hotel Ads; review signals directly influence organic and map ranking | ★★★ Essential |
| **Booking.com** | High-volume OTA; review score affects listing visibility and sort position within the platform | ★★★ Essential |
| **TripAdvisor** | Still influential for luxury and international travelers; feeds comparison aggregators | ★★ Important |
| **Expedia / Hotels.com** | Major for the US market and chain properties | ★★ Important |
| **Airbnb** | Growing relevance for boutique and design-forward independent properties | ★ Situational |

A practical note on platform dynamics: Booking.com and TripAdvisor collect their own reviews natively — you cannot redirect guests from those platforms to Google. Your strategy there is to respond well and steadily improve your score within each platform independently. Google is where you have the most control: you can send guests a direct link to your review page via email, SMS, or WhatsApp after every checkout, entirely on your own terms.

## How do hotels collect more guest reviews?

**The most reliable way to get more reviews is to ask at the right moment, through the right channel, with a frictionless single-link path to publish.**

Most hotels that struggle with review volume share one of three patterns: they don't ask at all, they ask at checkout (wrong moment, wrong headspace), or they send a generic email three days later when the experience has already faded from memory.

### Timing: the 4–24 hour window

The optimal window for a review request is **4 to 24 hours after checkout**. The stay is still fresh, the guest is settled, and they have a quiet moment to reflect and write. At checkout itself, guests are managing luggage, processing payment, and mentally already moving to the next thing — not in the right headspace to write a thoughtful review.

### Channel: match the guest's preference

| Channel | Relative response rate | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Post-stay **email** | Moderate | Guests with high email engagement, longer-stay profiles |
| Post-stay **SMS** | Higher | Mobile-first guests, same-day requests |
| **WhatsApp** | Highest | Markets where WhatsApp is the primary channel (EU, LATAM, Middle East, Africa) |

The rule: use the channel the guest already engaged on during their stay. A guest who received their pre-arrival confirmation by WhatsApp will respond to a post-stay request on WhatsApp. A guest who ignored your pre-stay SMS probably will ignore the review request there too.

### The ask: short, direct, one link

Keep the message to three sentences. Acknowledge the stay briefly. Ask if they'd be willing to share their experience. Provide a single direct link to your Google review page. Every additional step — navigating a website, finding the review tab, signing into an account — loses guests along the way. Friction kills conversion.

<Callout type="tip">
**Run a satisfaction filter before asking for a public review.** Send a short NPS-style question first ("On a scale of 1–10, how was your stay?"). Guests who score you 8–10 receive a direct link to your Google review page. Guests who score 6 or below receive a private feedback form — routing the issue to a manager *before* it becomes a public 2-star review. This is what separates a review management system from a basic post-stay email.
</Callout>

[Guest satisfaction surveys](/en/blog/experience-client/hotel-guest-satisfaction-survey) capture structured operational feedback for your internal team; review requests ask guests to publish publicly. Use both, in the right order: survey first to gauge sentiment, then route by score to the appropriate destination.

## How should hotels respond to positive reviews?

**Every positive review deserves a response — brief, specific, and human, not copy-pasted from a template.**

A response to a positive review isn't just politeness. It signals to every future reader that the hotel actually reads its feedback, that there is a real team behind the score. That visible attentiveness influences conversion: browsers weighing two similar properties often tip toward the one that responds to guests thoughtfully.

A strong positive response does three things:

1. **Acknowledges something specific** — references what the guest highlighted, not just "your kind review"
2. **Thanks sincerely** — one genuine sentence, not three exclamation marks
3. **Invites back** — a natural, warm close that leaves the door open for a return visit

**Weak response:**
> "Thank you so much for your wonderful 5-star review! We are so happy you enjoyed your stay and hope to see you again soon!"

**Strong response:**
> "Thank you, Sarah — we're really glad the corner suite exceeded expectations and that you made it to breakfast before the kitchen rush. The team will be delighted to hear it. We hope to see you again — if you're back in the autumn, the terrace is something quite special in November."

The second version takes thirty seconds more to write. It converts incomparably better because it shows a real person read the review.

## How should hotels respond to negative reviews?

**Respond to every negative review within 48 hours — publicly, calmly, and with specificity about the issue raised.**

<ImageBlock id="responding" />

Future guests read negative reviews. More importantly, they read how the hotel responds. A thoughtful reply to a 2-star review can do more for your booking conversion than ten generic 5-star responses — because it shows accountability and genuine professionalism rather than defensiveness.

### The response framework

| Step | What to say | What to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **Open** | Acknowledge the guest by name if possible; thank them for taking the time | "Dear Guest" (impersonal); defensive openers |
| **Acknowledge** | Name the specific problem — don't be vague or deflect | "Sorry you felt that way" (dismisses the experience) |
| **Explain (if relevant)** | Context is fine; excuses are not | Blaming staff, other guests, or circumstances |
| **Offer resolution** | Invite them to contact you directly to discuss | Making compensation offers publicly (sets a precedent) |
| **Close** | Express genuine intent to improve; wish them well | Promises you cannot keep |

<Callout type="warning">
**Never dispute a review publicly**, even if you are confident it is inaccurate or unfair. If a review violates platform guidelines — offensive language, proven false information — flag it for removal through the platform's official process. But do not argue in the comments. In every public exchange, the hotel that argues looks worse than the review itself.
</Callout>

### The real opportunity in negative reviews

Handled well, a critical review is one of the most useful data points you receive. It tells you what is broken before more guests experience the same issue. It demonstrates to future guests that you take feedback seriously — a meaningful differentiator in a market where most hotels either ignore bad reviews or respond defensively. And it feeds directly into operational improvement: the noise complaint that appears repeatedly is telling you something about room allocation, infrastructure, or check-in protocols that your team survey probably isn't surfacing.

Hotels that manage negative reviews systematically often see their overall rating improve faster than hotels that simply chase more 5-star responses — because response quality is a signal in platform algorithms, and because resolved operational issues stop generating new complaints.

## How do you automate hotel review management?

**Automate hotel review management by connecting your software to your PMS, then configuring a post-checkout sequence that triggers without any staff intervention.**

A well-configured automated workflow looks like this:

**Hour 0** — Guest checks out. The PMS event triggers the post-stay sequence automatically.

**Hour 4–6** — An automated message arrives via the guest's preferred channel (email, SMS, or WhatsApp) with a brief satisfaction check: one question, scale of 1–10.

**Routing (immediate)** — Score 8–10: the guest receives a direct link to your Google review page. Score 6 or below: the response routes to a private feedback form and the manager receives an alert.

**Day 2 (optional)** — If no response to the initial message, a single gentle follow-up nudge by the same channel.

**Day 7 (optional)** — For loyalty-enrolled guests: a short message with a direct booking incentive for their next visit.

The result: reviews accumulate systematically, unhappy guests are intercepted before they publish publicly, and your Google score builds without anyone on the team needing to remember to act after each checkout.

## How do reviews connect to your hotel's guest relationship?

**Every review is a data point — and the hotels that treat it as one, rather than just a score to manage, gain a compounding commercial advantage.**

A guest who leaves a glowing 5-star review has just told you something valuable: they had an excellent experience. They are your most likely candidate for a direct rebooking, your warmest audience for a loyalty offer, and your most credible word-of-mouth asset. If that guest disappears into a spreadsheet after checkout rather than into a [hotel CRM](/en/blog/experience-client/hotel-crm-guide) segment labelled "advocates," a significant repeat revenue opportunity goes untapped.

A guest who leaves a 3-star review with specific feedback is telling you something even more valuable: here is precisely what to fix. That feedback, routed privately before publication, gives your operations team an early signal — the kind that prevents the same complaint from appearing in twelve more reviews before anyone addresses the root cause.

Both outcomes require a connected system. Review management disconnected from the guest relationship — a standalone tool that sends requests and collects responses but never enriches a CRM profile — captures the score while losing the insight.

For hotels working to reduce OTA dependency, the connection matters financially too. Each direct rebooking avoids the commission OTAs charge — industry-typically 15–20% of the booking value. A guest you recognise, remember, and stay in touch with is a guest who books direct next time. The [CRM](/en/produit/crm) is what makes that loop systematic rather than accidental, and review management is the first step in building a relationship that lasts beyond the stay.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many Google reviews does a hotel need?**

There is no magic target number, but recent volume matters more than total count. A hotel with 80 reviews in the last six months outperforms one with 500 reviews from three years ago — both in guest perception and in Google's local ranking algorithm, which weights recency heavily. Building a steady, consistent flow is more valuable than chasing a round-number milestone.

---

**Can I ask a guest to delete a negative review?**

You can ask — guests are free to edit or remove their own reviews. Most won't, and the request can backfire if it comes across as pressure. A more reliable approach: respond thoughtfully in public, resolve the issue privately, and let a genuine resolution speak for itself. Guests who feel truly heard sometimes update their review voluntarily; that outcome is far better than one obtained under pressure.

---

**What is the best time to send a hotel review request?**

4 to 24 hours post-checkout. The stay is still fresh, the guest is settled, and they have a quiet moment to reflect and write. At checkout itself, guests are managing luggage, processing payments, and thinking about transport — not the right headspace for a thoughtful review. Within the 24-hour window, the specific timing matters less than hitting it.

---

**Should I respond to every hotel review?**

Yes — every negative review without exception, and as many positive reviews as your team can respond to with genuine specificity. Prioritise negative reviews for speed (aim within 48 hours); prioritise positive reviews for personalization. A generic "thank you for your kind review" adds limited value; a response that references something specific from the stay builds real trust with future readers.

---

**Does responding to reviews help Google ranking?**

Google recommends responding to reviews, and it's widely considered a positive local SEO signal — it reflects active property management, which search engines tend to reward. There's no confirmed direct ranking boost. The direct ranking effect is modest; the indirect conversion effect is often more significant. Future guests who see a hotel responding thoughtfully to every review gain confidence that the property is attentive and accountable — and that trust influences the booking decision.

---

**What is the difference between a guest satisfaction survey and a review request?**

A [guest satisfaction survey](/en/blog/experience-client/hotel-guest-satisfaction-survey) is a structured private questionnaire that captures operational feedback for your internal team — what service failed, what amenity fell short, what process needs fixing. A review request asks guests to publish their opinion publicly. The two are complementary and should run in sequence: survey first to gauge sentiment, then use the satisfaction score to decide which guests to route toward a public review platform and which to handle privately.
