## What is automated guest feedback collection?

Automated guest feedback collection is software-triggered outreach that sends a rating or survey request to a guest automatically when they check out — no manual action required from your team. The PMS fires a checkout event; the platform sends the message; the response comes back and gets routed based on the score.

The core components:

- **Trigger**: checkout event from your PMS (Mews, Opera Cloud, Medialog, Thais, Misterbooking, and more added regularly)
- **Channel**: email, SMS, or WhatsApp — or a sequence of both
- **Message**: a short survey, an NPS rating question, or a direct link to a feedback form
- **Routing logic**: high score → prompt to leave a public review; low score → internal alert to the duty manager

Everything that used to require a staff member to remember, locate the contact, and write the message happens automatically, for every guest, within minutes of checkout.

## Why does automating guest feedback matter for hotels?

Automation matters because timing and consistency are the two things manual processes can't reliably deliver.

**Timing** is the first issue. Guests remember their stay most clearly in the first few hours after checkout — when the experience is still fresh and emotions are still present. The longer you wait, the more the details blur. An automated message sent two hours after checkout reaches a guest while they're still thinking about the room, the breakfast, the service. A follow-up sent three days later — or never — doesn't.

**Consistency** is the second issue. When feedback collection depends on staff initiative, it's inconsistent by design. Busy checkout desk? No follow-up. Short-staffed weekend? No follow-up. Difficult guest interaction? Definitely no follow-up. Automation removes that variability: every departure triggers the same process.

**Volume** follows from consistency. More guests asked means more feedback received and better data for decisions. At the scale TriggerFlow operates, <Stat id="messages" /> messages sent through the platform represent the kind of volume that reveals real patterns, not noise.

**Speed** matters most for damage control. An unhappy guest who receives a private resolution channel within two hours is far less likely to post publicly than one who receives nothing for 72 hours and has already moved on.

|---|---|---|
| **Consistency** | Depends on staff | 100% of departures |
| **Timing** | Days later (or never) | Hours after checkout |
| **Volume** | Low | High |
| **Actionability** | Reactive | Proactive |
| **Staff effort** | High | Near zero |

## What channels work best for collecting hotel guest feedback?

The best channel is the one your guests actually open — and that depends on your property type, guest demographics, and the consents you've collected.

<ImageBlock id="feedback-channels" />

| Channel | Strengths | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Email** | High reach, works for transactional messages without extra opt-in, suitable for longer surveys | All guest segments, detailed feedback |
| **SMS** | Very high open rate, seen within minutes of sending, short and direct | Time-sensitive NPS routing, guests who rarely check email |
| **WhatsApp** | Highest engagement, feels personal, two-way conversation possible | Loyal guests, upscale or boutique properties (requires opt-in) |

## When should hotels send an automated feedback request?

Between one and two hours after checkout is the sweet spot for most properties — the guest has had time to settle from the rush, but the stay is still vivid.

| Timing | Response quality | Key consideration |
|---|---|---|
| At check-out kiosk | Very high | Can feel rushed or performative |
| **1–2 hours after checkout** | **High** | **Sweet spot for most hotels** |
| Same evening | Good | Works well for morning departures |
| 24 hours later | Moderate | Recall starting to fade |
| 48–72 hours later | Low | Not recommended |

<Callout type="warning">
The feedback window closes faster than most teams realize. After 24 hours, recall fades and response rates drop — and unhappy guests who received no outreach may have already posted publicly. Tight timing requires real-time PMS synchronization. If your integration runs on a daily batch sync, you've already missed the window for same-day departures.
</Callout>

## How do you set up automated guest feedback collection?

Setting up automated feedback takes hours, not weeks — provided your platform integrates natively with your PMS.

<ImageBlock id="automation-flow" />

### Step 1 — Connect your PMS

Your PMS is the trigger source. When a guest checks out, the PMS fires an event; your CRM or guest-experience platform picks it up and initiates the workflow. Native integrations with Mews, Opera Cloud, Medialog, Thais, Misterbooking, and more added regularly mean no manual data export and no delay. The guest's name, email, phone, and booking details are already in the system. For a deeper look at how PMS and CRM work together, the [hotel CRM guide](/en/blog/experience-client/hotel-crm-guide) covers the integration mechanics in full.

### Step 2 — Design your feedback form

Short forms get completed; long ones get abandoned. A single NPS or 1–5 star rating question combined with one open-text field consistently outperforms multi-page surveys for completion rate. For questions that generate actionable insight, see [the right survey questions to ask hotel guests](/en/blog/experience-client/hotel-guest-satisfaction-survey-questions). For the full methodology, read [the complete guide to hotel guest satisfaction surveys](/en/blog/experience-client/hotel-guest-satisfaction-survey). [TriggerFlow's feedback forms module](/en/produit/formulaires) includes ready-to-use templates you can customize without building anything from scratch.

### Step 3 — Choose your channel and timing

Set email as your primary channel. Define your send window — for example, two hours after checkout, between 8 am and 9 pm. Add SMS for guests who haven't opened the email after a set delay, if you have their mobile number. Configure WhatsApp for opted-in guests who prefer messaging.

### Step 4 — Configure routing logic

Set a score threshold — typically NPS 8–10 or a 4–5 star rating. Guests above it receive a prompt to share their experience publicly on Google or Booking.com, with a direct link. Guests below it trigger an internal alert to the duty manager, or a follow-up message offering to resolve the issue privately. This is the step that turns feedback collection into reputation protection.

### Step 5 — Monitor and iterate

Review open rates, completion rates, and score distributions monthly. If completion is low, shorten the form. If timing feels off, adjust the delay. The workflow is set-and-forget for daily operations but benefits from a quarterly review.

<Callout type="tip">
The most common mistake is over-engineering the first version. Start with one channel, one question, one routing rule. Add complexity once you've seen the baseline and know what's working.
</Callout>

## How does automated feedback connect to your hotel's online reputation?

Automated feedback collection and online reputation management are not two separate workflows — they're two stages of the same one.

The connection is NPS routing. When a guest rates their stay above your threshold, the next automated message — sent seconds after submission — invites them to share that positive experience on Google or Booking.com, with a direct link that removes every friction point. When a guest rates below the threshold, no public review link goes out. Instead, the team receives an internal alert and can reach out directly.

Happy guests who might otherwise have gone home and said nothing are actively routed toward public platforms at the moment they're most likely to act. Unhappy guests get a private resolution channel before they've had time to reach for their keyboard in frustration.

## Frequently asked questions about automating hotel guest feedback

**What is the best time to send a post-stay feedback request?**

Between one and two hours after checkout is the sweet spot for most properties. The stay is still fresh, the guest has had time to decompress from the checkout process, and the emotional impression of the experience is intact. Waiting until the next day sees both response rates and feedback quality decline steadily.

---

**Can I automate feedback collection without a PMS integration?**

Technically yes — but you lose the primary benefit. Without PMS sync, feedback requests have to be triggered manually or via scheduled list uploads, which reintroduces the inconsistency and timing gaps that automation is meant to eliminate. A native PMS integration is what makes real-time triggering reliable at scale.

---

**What's the difference between a guest feedback form and a review request?**

A feedback form captures private input — ratings, open comments, specific service scores — that goes to your internal team. A review request directs the guest to a public platform (Google, Booking.com, TripAdvisor) to share their experience publicly. Best practice is to do both in sequence: collect private feedback first, then route guests who score above your threshold toward a public review.

---

**How can automated feedback help prevent bad public reviews?**

NPS routing is the mechanism. Guests who rate their stay below your threshold receive a message from the team offering to address their concern — a private resolution path. Most guests who feel heard and responded to don't go on to publish a negative review. The key is speed: the follow-up needs to happen while the guest still considers the matter open, which is why same-day automated triggering matters.

---

**Does feedback automation work for one-night stays?**

Especially for one-night stays. Guests with a single-night experience have a compressed memory window — there's no multi-day arc to smooth over a rough moment. Getting their feedback quickly matters most for this segment. Automation ensures a one-night guest gets the same follow-up process as a week-long stay, something manual processes almost never achieve.
