Most hotels do collect guest feedback. What they don't do is collect it *consistently*.

A receptionist remembers to ask after a smooth check-out. Nobody remembers during a busy Saturday turnover. The post-stay email sits in a template folder, sent maybe twice a month when someone thinks of it. And the guest who had a real problem — the one whose feedback mattered most — has already written a public review and moved on.

**Inconsistency is the real enemy of guest feedback.** Automation fixes that — not by replacing the human relationship, but by making sure the system never forgets what the human sometimes does. Whether you are new to the concept or replacing a manual process that has stopped scaling, the setup is simpler than most teams expect.

## What is automated guest feedback collection?

Automated guest feedback collection means setting up event-based triggers — moments tied to a guest's journey, like check-out or a specific time during the stay — that send a satisfaction survey or check-in message automatically, without manual action from your team.

Instead of relying on a receptionist to remember, the platform watches for the trigger (check-out logged in the PMS, for example), and fires the right message — by [email](/en/produit/email), [SMS](/en/produit/sms), or [WhatsApp](/en/produit/whatsapp) — to the right guest at the right moment. These automated guest surveys reach every departing guest, not just those who checked out during a quiet weekday morning.

The survey itself can be as simple as a one-question NPS or as structured as a multi-section [guest satisfaction questionnaire](/en/blog/experience-client/hotel-guest-satisfaction-survey). What matters is that it goes out every time — not just when someone remembers.

## Why should hotels automate guest feedback collection?

Hotels that automate feedback collection get more responses, catch problems earlier, and protect their online reputation consistently — without adding work to the team.

Here is what manual feedback collection actually costs:

| Problem with manual collection | Impact |
|---|---|
| Inconsistency (depends on who is working) | Patchy data, biased toward easy interactions |
| Delay (surveys sent days after check-out) | Response rates drop sharply past 24–48 hours |
| No routing (happy and unhappy guests get the same link) | Complaints that could have been resolved privately go public |
| Team burden (someone has to remember and send) | It simply does not happen during busy periods |

Automating feedback collection solves all four: every guest is contacted, at the optimal moment, through the right channel — with unhappy guests routed to private resolution before a public review is written.

<Callout type="tip">
Response rates for post-stay surveys drop by roughly half after 48 hours — an industry-typical pattern well documented in hospitality research. Automation is the only reliable way to hit that window for every single check-out.
</Callout>

## When is the best time to collect guest feedback?

The best time to collect guest feedback depends on what you need to learn — different moments in the stay surface different information, and a complete strategy covers all three.

<ImageBlock id="feedback-timing" />

### Before arrival — listening before the stay begins

A short pre-arrival form, sent 2–3 days before check-in, captures preferences and special requests. This is less "feedback" and more proactive listening. Use it to surface dietary requirements, accessibility needs, room preferences, or celebration occasions — information that lets your team personalize the welcome before the guest walks through the door.

This data flows directly into the guest profile in your [hotel CRM](/en/produit/crm), enriching it before a single interaction has taken place.

### During the stay — your safety net

An in-stay check-in message, sent 12–24 hours after arrival for stays of two nights or more, asks one simple question: *"Is everything going well? Anything we can improve?"*

This is where you prevent bad reviews before they happen. A guest with a noisy room or a broken shower will tell you now — when you can still fix it — rather than on TripAdvisor after they leave. For multi-night stays, this single touchpoint typically has the highest return of any automated message you can send.

### After check-out — the core satisfaction survey

The post-stay survey is the anchor of any feedback strategy. Triggered within 1–4 hours of check-out (while the experience is still fresh), it covers a fuller assessment: room quality, service, breakfast, overall impression, and likelihood to return.

| Moment | Trigger | Best channel | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-arrival | 2–3 days before check-in | Email | Capture preferences, personalize welcome |
| In-stay | 12–24 h after check-in (2+ nights) | SMS or WhatsApp | Catch problems before check-out |
| Post-stay | 1–4 h after check-out | Email + SMS follow-up | Collect full satisfaction data |

<Callout type="warning">
Do not send all three touchpoints to every guest indiscriminately. For a one-night stay, skip the in-stay message. For a transient business traveler, a detailed pre-arrival form may feel intrusive. Good automation platforms let you set these conditions — guests receive what is relevant, not a flood of requests.
</Callout>

## How do you set up automated guest feedback collection — step by step?

Setting up a guest feedback automation takes a few hours with a modern platform. Here is the practical sequence.

**Step 1 — Connect your PMS.** Your automation platform needs real-time access to check-out and check-in events. Without a native PMS integration, the trigger doesn't fire reliably. Confirm this integration exists and runs in real time — not as a nightly batch — before you evaluate anything else. TriggerFlow connects natively to [Mews](/en/integrations/pms/mews) and other leading PMS used across European independent hotels.

**Step 2 — Design your survey.** Start simple: one overall satisfaction question (NPS scale or star rating), then 2–4 specific questions covering room, service, and breakfast. Resist the urge to ask 20 things. Response rates fall sharply as length increases. The [hotel satisfaction survey questions guide](/en/blog/experience-client/hotel-guest-satisfaction-survey-questions) has tested formats and ready-to-use templates.

**Step 3 — Set your triggers.** Define the rules: *post-stay survey fires 2 hours after check-out; in-stay check-in fires 18 hours after check-in for stays of 2+ nights; pre-arrival preference form fires 48 hours before arrival.* Most platforms expose these in a visual editor — no code required.

**Step 4 — Choose your channels.** Email is the baseline for post-stay (guests expect it). SMS adds roughly 30–40 percentage points of open-rate improvement over email alone — an industry-typical result. WhatsApp delivers the highest engagement where guests already have the app. Match the channel to the guest type: business travelers on email, leisure guests on SMS or WhatsApp.

**Step 5 — Configure routing logic.** This is the step most hotels skip, and the one that changes the trajectory of your review score. Set a score threshold: guests who rate 8 or above (out of 10) receive a follow-up inviting them to post a public review on [Google or Booking.com](/en/blog/experience-client/google-booking-reviews-hotel). Guests who score below that threshold receive a private form and an automatic alert goes to your manager. One configuration, two completely different guest journeys.

**Step 6 — Train your team on the dashboard.** Automation handles the sending; a human still needs to own the private escalation inbox and respond to unhappy guests within a defined window. Automation without a human on the other end of the process is incomplete.

**Step 7 — Review and iterate after 30 days.** Check send rate, open rate, response rate, NPS trend, and review conversion. If response rates are low, the survey is too long or the timing is off. If review conversion is near zero, the routing message needs rewriting. Adjust one variable at a time.

## How does automated feedback connect to online reviews?

Automated feedback collection is half the system. The other half is what you do with positive responses.

<ImageBlock id="review-routing" />

The pattern that compounds results is called **sentiment routing** (or NPS routing):

1. Guest completes the post-stay survey.
2. The platform reads the score automatically.
3. **Promoters (high score):** receive a follow-up with a direct, friction-reduced link to Google, Booking.com, or TripAdvisor.
4. **Detractors (low score):** receive a private message — *"We are sorry your stay did not fully meet expectations. Can you share more?"* — routed to a manager, never to a public review platform.

This is not manipulating reviews. It is recovering service failures in private (where you can act on them) and amplifying genuine satisfaction publicly (where it helps future guests decide to book). The net result: review volume grows, your average score improves, and problems that would have become two-star ratings get resolved before they are published.

## What metrics should you track to measure feedback automation performance?

Five numbers tell you whether the system is working, and what to fix when it isn't.

| Metric | What it measures | Healthy benchmark |
|---|---|---|
| **Survey send rate** | % of check-outs that triggered a survey | Should be at or near 100% |
| **Open rate** | % of sent surveys opened by the guest | Email: 40–60%; SMS: 80%+ |
| **Response rate** | % of opened surveys completed | 25–50% for a short, well-timed survey |
| **NPS or average score** | Overall satisfaction level | Track the trend month over month |
| **Review conversion rate** | % of promoters who post a public review | 15–30% is realistic with a frictionless direct link |

If send rate is below 95%, the PMS integration is dropping check-out events — investigate first. If response rates are low, the survey is too long or sent too late. If review conversion hovers near zero, rewrite the routing message and test a more direct call to action.

**What is the difference between automated feedback collection and a regular guest survey?**

A regular guest survey is a form you send manually when someone remembers. **Which channel gets the best response rates for post-stay surveys?**

SMS and WhatsApp consistently outperform email on open rates — 80%+ versus 40–60% for email, which is an industry-typical pattern. However, email handles richer survey formats better. Best practice: send a short SMS or WhatsApp prompt with a link to the survey hosted online. For guests who do not click, an email follow-up the next morning recovers additional responses without feeling intrusive.

**How long should a hotel post-stay survey be?**

Three to five questions is the sweet spot. One overall satisfaction question (NPS scale or star rating) plus two to four specific questions covering room, service, food and beverage, and likelihood to return. Beyond five questions, completion rates drop sharply. This single configuration, applied consistently to every departure, is the highest-impact element of any feedback automation system.

**How does GDPR apply to automated guest feedback collection in Europe?**

You need a valid legal basis to contact guests post-stay. For satisfaction surveys, this is typically legitimate interest (short, non-commercial, directly related to the stay) or the performance of the contract for in-stay service communications. Guests must be able to opt out of marketing messages. A compliant platform stores data on European servers, logs consent, and allows guest data to be exported or deleted on request. Always verify data residency before deploying any guest messaging platform on European guests.
