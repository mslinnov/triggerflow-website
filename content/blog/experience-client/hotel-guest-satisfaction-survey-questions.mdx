**In short:** The right hotel satisfaction survey questions — asked at the right stay stage — turn vague impressions into specific, actionable feedback. This guide gives you 40+ ready-to-use questions organized by pre-arrival, in-stay, and post-stay, plus best practices for length, rating scales, and automated collection.
</Callout>

Most hotel satisfaction surveys fail before guests even read them.

Not because the questions are bad — but because there are too many of them, they're sent at the wrong moment, or the results sit in a spreadsheet nobody checks. A well-designed hotel guest satisfaction survey is short, timed to the stay stage, and connected to an action: routing a happy guest to Google, alerting a manager to a live complaint, or enriching a guest profile in your [hotel CRM](/en/blog/experience-client/hotel-crm-guide).

Here is everything you need to build one.

---

## What makes a good hotel guest satisfaction survey question?

A good hotel guest satisfaction survey question is **specific, easy to answer, and tied to something your team can act on**. It should take under 30 seconds to respond to and point clearly to a department or process that can change.

Four criteria separate useful questions from filler:

1. **Specificity:** "How satisfied were you with breakfast?" tells you more than "How was your overall experience?"
2. **Actionability:** Every question should trace back to a team, a process, or a decision your hotel can actually make.
3. **Brevity:** Each additional question reduces completion rates. Every slot is a cost — protect it.
4. **Stage relevance:** A question about check-out experience is worthless in a mid-stay survey. Questions should cover only what the guest has already experienced.

---

## How should you structure a hotel satisfaction survey?

**Structure your hotel satisfaction survey around three separate touchpoints** — each focused only on the stage the guest has just lived through.

| Survey stage | When to send | What to ask | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pre-arrival** | 48–72h before check-in | Preferences, ETA, special requests | Personalize the arrival |
| **In-stay** | Night 2, or 50% of stay | Room, comfort, live issues | Fix problems before checkout |
| **Post-stay** | Within 2h of checkout | Full experience, NPS, return intent | Collect data, route reviews |

Most hotels send only one post-stay survey. Adding a mid-stay pulse typically catches 30–40% of fixable problems before they become negative reviews.

<Callout type="tip">
**Never combine stages.** A pre-arrival form that asks about the stay experience, or a post-stay survey that asks about room preferences, confuses guests and tanks your completion rate. One form per stage, focused only on that stage.
</Callout>

## Pre-arrival survey questions (Template 1)

Send this 48–72 hours before check-in, embedded in the pre-arrival email. **Keep it to 3–5 questions maximum.**

**Purpose:** Gather preferences so the stay starts right.

1. **What is the main purpose of your stay?** *(Business / Leisure / Special occasion / Other)*
2. **What time do you expect to arrive?** *(Before noon / Afternoon / Evening / Late evening)*
3. **Do you have any room preferences we should try to accommodate?** *(Free text — optional)*
4. **Are there any dietary restrictions or allergies our team should know about?** *(Yes — please describe / No)*
5. **Is there anything we can prepare in advance to make your stay special?** *(Free text — optional)*

When your [hotel CRM](/en/blog/experience-client/hotel-crm-guide) is connected to your PMS, answers push directly into the guest profile and surface at check-in — without the receptionist searching through emails.

---

## In-stay survey questions (Template 2)

Send on day 2 for multi-night stays, or at the 50% point for shorter trips. **Limit to 3 questions** — guests are on holiday, not filling in a report.

**Purpose:** Catch and fix problems while the guest is still on property.

6. **Overall, how is your stay going so far?** *(1–5 stars or emoji scale)*
7. **Is there anything about your room or your stay we can improve right now?** *(Free text)*
8. **Is there anything else our team can help you with before checkout?** *(Free text — optional)*

<Callout type="warning">
The in-stay survey is only useful if it triggers an alert. If a guest says their room is too hot, someone needs to see that within minutes — not when a manager reviews the weekly report. Wire every low score to an immediate notification.
</Callout>

---

## Post-stay survey questions — the complete template

**Post-stay is the survey that matters most for your reputation and your data.** Send it within 2 hours of checkout while the experience is still fresh. Aim for **7–10 questions**.

### Core experience

9. **Overall, how would you rate your stay?** *(1–10)*
10. **How would you rate the cleanliness of your room and the hotel?** *(1–5)*
11. **How would you rate the friendliness and helpfulness of our team?** *(1–5)*
12. **How would you rate the check-in experience?** *(1–5)*
13. **How would you rate the check-out experience?** *(1–5)*
14. **Was the hotel as described at the time of booking?** *(Yes / Mostly yes / No)*
15. **Did anything fall short of your expectations during your stay?** *(Free text — optional)*

### Specific services (include only what applies to your property)

16. **How would you rate the quality of our breakfast or restaurant?** *(1–5 / N/A)*
17. **How would you rate our bar or lounge?** *(1–5 / N/A)*
18. **How would you rate our pool, spa, or wellness facilities?** *(1–5 / N/A)*
19. **How would you rate the in-room amenities (toiletries, coffee, etc.)?** *(1–5)*
20. **Was the hotel quiet enough for a restful stay?** *(Yes / Somewhat / No)*
21. **How would you rate the quality of our beds and linen?** *(1–5)*
22. **How would you rate our Wi-Fi connection?** *(1–5)*

### Loyalty and return intent

23. **How likely are you to recommend our hotel to a friend or colleague?** *(0–10 — your NPS question)*
24. **How likely are you to return to our hotel?** *(Very likely / Likely / Unlikely / Very unlikely)*
25. **Did you book directly with us or through an OTA?** *(Direct / OTA)*
26. **Would you like to receive exclusive offers from our hotel?** *(Yes + email field / No)*
27. **What was the highlight of your stay?** *(Free text — optional)*
28. **Is there anything that would make you choose us again for your next trip?** *(Free text — optional)*

<Callout type="info">
**Quick-start template:** For a focused 8-question post-stay survey, use questions 9, 11, 13, 15, 20, 23, 24, and 26. That covers overall satisfaction, staff, checkout, issues, quiet, NPS, return intent, and direct opt-in — in under 2 minutes for the guest.
</Callout>

## Bonus: specialty and contextual questions

Add 2–3 of these for boutique, eco, wellness, or family hotels — replacing lower-priority standard questions rather than stacking on top.

29. **Did our team anticipate your needs without being asked?** *(Yes / Partially / No)*
30. **Did you feel our sustainability commitments were visible during your stay?** *(Yes / Somewhat / No)*
31. **Were our local recommendations and activity suggestions helpful?** *(Yes / No / Didn't ask)*
32. **How well did we accommodate your family's needs?** *(1–5 / N/A)*
33. **Did you use our mobile check-in or digital key?** *(Yes — rate 1–5 / No)*
34. **How would you rate the parking or valet service?** *(1–5 / N/A)*
35. **Did you use room service or in-room dining?** *(Yes — rate 1–5 / No)*

## Post-stay NPS deep-dive questions

For multi-property groups running NPS as a standalone program, these five complement the standard loyalty block:

36. **On a scale of 0–10, how likely are you to recommend [Hotel Name] to someone you know?** *(core NPS question)*
37. **What is the main reason for your score?** *(Free text — critical: without this, NPS gives you a number, not a direction)*
38. **Compared to other hotels at a similar price point, how did we perform?** *(Better / Similar / Worse)*
39. **Which one thing would have most improved your stay?** *(Free text)*
40. **If you gave us a low score, would you be willing to speak with a manager?** *(Yes / No)*

## How many questions should a hotel guest survey include?

**Keep post-stay surveys to 7–10 questions.** Completion rates drop sharply above 10 — and a 40% completion rate on a 10-question survey gives you more usable data than a 7% rate on a 25-question one.

| Survey type | Recommended length | Target completion rate |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-arrival | 3–5 questions | 60–70% |
| In-stay pulse | 2–3 questions | 50–65% |
| Post-stay (standard) | 7–10 questions | 35–55% |
| Post-stay (incentivized) | 12–15 questions | 20–35% |

Offering a small incentive — loyalty points or a discount on the next stay — can lift completion rates on longer surveys by 15–25 percentage points, but only if your loyalty program is wired to redeem it automatically on response.

---

## What rating scale works best for hotel guest surveys?

**The best hotel survey rating scale depends on what you want to do with the data.** Three formats dominate hospitality:

| Scale | Best for | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| **1–5 stars** | Department-level ratings (room, staff, breakfast) | Harder to benchmark across brands |
| **1–10 numeric** | Overall satisfaction, multi-property benchmarking | Slightly more cognitive load |
| **NPS (0–10)** | Loyalty and word-of-mouth intent | Needs a free-text "why?" to be actionable |
| **Emoji / sentiment** | In-stay micro-surveys | Low granularity for operational use |

<Callout type="tip">
**Practical pick:** Use 1–5 stars for department questions, a 1–10 scale for overall satisfaction, and a 0–10 NPS question for return and referral intent. Three complementary data points from one survey.
</Callout>

## How do you turn survey results into action?

**Survey data creates no value until it's routed to the right person or system automatically.** Three flows cover 90% of what matters.

### Route happy guests to public reviews

A guest who scores 9 or 10 on overall satisfaction is a natural advocate. If your survey flow immediately redirects them to your Google review page after submission, you convert passive satisfaction into a public endorsement — without asking twice.

Hotels using [TriggerFlow's review routing](/en/produit/avis) report a **<Stat id="googleReviews" /> increase in Google reviews** after enabling this flow. More reviews mean a higher average score (you're finally capturing the satisfied guests who previously said nothing), and higher scores directly improve visibility on Google Maps.

→ [How to get more Google & Booking.com reviews for your hotel](/en/blog/experience-client/google-booking-reviews-hotel)

### Flag unhappy guests before they publish

A guest who scores 5 or below should trigger an immediate manager alert — not a review request. A personal call or message within a few hours recovers far more guest relationships than any public reply to a bad review later. Threshold-based routing rules are a standard feature of any serious hotel survey platform.

### Feed results into guest profiles

Every survey response that lands in a [hotel CRM](/en/blog/experience-client/hotel-crm-guide) enriches the guest record. A guest who rated breakfast 2/5 should never receive a "breakfast upgrade" upsell on their next stay. A guest who rated the spa 5/5 is exactly who to target with the next wellness offer. The survey is not only a reputation tool — it is a data source.

## How to automate hotel guest satisfaction surveys

**Manual survey sending does not scale.** If checkout is busy and the message goes out 48 hours late, your response rate halves. The right setup triggers surveys automatically from PMS events, with no human step required.

A standard four-step automation flow:

1. **Checkout event in PMS** → post-stay survey sent within 2 hours
2. **Mid-stay trigger** (night 2 or 50% of stay) → in-stay pulse sent automatically
3. **Pre-arrival trigger** (72h before check-in) → preferences form sent
4. **Survey response received** → score ≥ 9: redirect to Google review page; score ≤ 5: alert duty manager immediately

## Frequently asked questions

**When is the best time to send a hotel guest satisfaction survey?**

Post-stay surveys perform best within 2 hours of checkout — open rates and recall are at their peak when the experience is fresh. After 24 hours, response rates drop significantly. In-stay surveys should go at the midpoint of the stay (night 2 for a 4-night stay), not on arrival when guests have not yet formed an opinion.

---

**How long should a hotel satisfaction survey be?**

7–10 questions for post-stay; 2–3 for an in-stay pulse; 3–5 for pre-arrival. Going above 10 questions in a post-stay survey typically halves completion rates. Every question you cut raises the quality of answers to the ones you keep.

---

**What is an NPS question in a hotel survey?**

NPS (Net Promoter Score) is the question: *"On a scale of 0–10, how likely are you to recommend this hotel to a friend or colleague?"* Scores of 9–10 are Promoters, 7–8 are Passives, 0–6 are Detractors. Your NPS = % Promoters minus % Detractors. It is useful for tracking loyalty trends over time — but always pair it with a free-text "why?" question, otherwise the number gives you no direction on what to fix.

---

**Should I use a 5-star or 10-point scale for hotel surveys?**

Use 1–5 stars for department-level questions — fast and intuitive for guests. Use a 10-point scale for overall satisfaction and NPS, which gives you finer segmentation to distinguish at-risk guests (6–7) from strong advocates (9–10). Both, applied to the right questions in the same survey, is the practical answer.

---

**What survey questions should a boutique hotel prioritize?**

Boutique hotels should weight questions around personalization (did the team anticipate your needs without being asked?), atmosphere (did the property match your expectations?), and local recommendations — the dimensions where boutique hotels compete, not thread count or parking capacity.

---

**How do I stop unhappy guests from leaving bad reviews?**

Route low scorers to a direct manager contact, not to a review platform. A threshold-based routing rule — overall score ≤ 6 → alert duty manager immediately — catches dissatisfied guests before they reach Google. A personal follow-up within a few hours is far more effective than responding publicly to a 1-star review after the fact.
