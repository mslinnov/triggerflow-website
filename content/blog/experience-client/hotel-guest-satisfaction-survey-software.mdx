Most hotels start their survey by hand. After a complaint, someone sends a Google Form, a dozen guests answer and the results end up in a spreadsheet. Then August arrives, reception is short of staff, and nobody sends it again.

The fix is not a better form. You need a survey that leaves on its own every morning, reaches the right guest in the right language, and warns a person when something went wrong. And you need to watch out for one compliance trap that some vendors are happy to sell you.

## What hotel guest satisfaction survey software actually does

Hotel guest satisfaction survey software is a tool that sends a questionnaire to guests at a set moment after their stay, collects the answers and turns them into scores and alerts the team can act on. In practice the job has four steps:

- **Trigger:** the survey leaves automatically, based on the check-out date in the reservation.
- **Collect:** the guest answers by email, SMS, WhatsApp or a QR code.
- **Analyse:** scores are broken down by period, room type or booking channel.
- **Act:** a low score raises an alert, and someone follows up.

It is not a review site. It is not a one-off form sent by hand after a complaint either. Both have their uses, but neither runs on its own. Hotels usually start shopping for software after the manual survey has died quietly for the second or third time, with its results in a file nobody opens.

## Three types of tools, and who each one suits

Survey tools for hotels fall into three categories: generic form builders, standalone hotel feedback platforms, and hotel CRMs with built-in surveys. The real difference between them is how close they sit to your reservation data.

<ImageBlock id="tool-types" />

| | Generic form builder | Standalone feedback platform | Hotel CRM with surveys |
|---|---|---|---|
| Automatic trigger from stay data | No | Yes, after setup | Yes |
| PMS link | None | Integration project, sometimes CSV | Native sync |
| Guest history | No | Kept in the platform, separate from the guest profile | Answer stored in the guest profile |
| Review invitation | Manual | Varies by vendor | Built into the flow |
| Alerts | Basic email notification at best | Yes | Yes |
| Typical cost model | Free or low subscription | Often quote-only | Public plans |
| Best for | A 10-room property testing the idea | Larger operations that need deep benchmarking | Independents who want it to run daily |

**Generic form builders** (Google Forms or Typeform style) cost little or nothing. But they know nothing about the reservation: nothing triggers them at check-out, and the answer arrives without a guest name or room number. For a 10-room property testing the idea, that is acceptable.

**Standalone feedback platforms** are strong on analytics and benchmarking. The price is usually quote-only, you have to run an integration project to set them up, and the answers live apart from the guest profile.

**A hotel CRM with built-in surveys** triggers the survey from the stay itself and stores the answer in the guest profile. From there, the answer can drive the next pre-arrival email, a segment or a loyalty offer. The <InternalLink href="/en/blog/experience-client/hotel-crm-guide">hotel CRM guide</InternalLink> explains how those pieces connect.

A rule of thumb from the field: the fewer systems between the PMS and the survey, the more likely the survey still runs in six months. Every export, import or manual step is a place where it can quietly stop.

## The criteria that decide the purchase

Eight criteria separate a survey tool that gets used from one that gets abandoned, and you can check all of them in one vendor call. Keep this table open during demos.

| Criterion | What to ask the vendor | Red flag |
|---|---|---|
| PMS integration | Which PMS exactly? Native sync or CSV import? | "We can work something out" |
| Send automation and timing | Is the delay configurable per segment? | Fixed delay, or manual sending |
| Channels | Email, SMS, WhatsApp, all in one flow? | Email only |
| Question editor and templates | Can I edit templates and keep them short? | Fixed questionnaire |
| Multilingual surveys | Does the survey follow the guest language from the reservation? | One language per account |
| Alerts on low scores | Who is alerted, how fast, through which channel? | A dashboard only, no alert |
| Reporting | Can I filter by period, room type and segment? | One global average |
| Price transparency | Setup fee, minimum rooms, lock-in, per-response fees? | "Contact us for a quote" |

Four of them cause most of the regrets.

**PMS integration.** Ask for the exact list of supported PMS and whether the sync is native or relies on a file import. TriggerFlow connects natively to Mews, Thaïs, Opera Cloud, Medialog, Misterbooking, Asterio and Apaleo.

**Language and timing.** A European independent hotel often welcomes guests from several countries in the same week, so the survey should go out in the language recorded in the reservation, never one picked by hand. The common setup is to send the day after check-out with one reminder. Make sure the delay can change per segment: a business traveller back at the office on Monday answers differently from a family still driving home.

**Pricing.** Look for setup fees, a minimum room count, an annual lock-in and per-response pricing. Any one of these can make a cheap-looking tool expensive. TriggerFlow publishes its plans on the <InternalLink href="/en/tarifs">pricing page</InternalLink>.

**Exportability.** Ask whether you can take your data with you if you leave. Without an export, you lose every past score the day you switch, along with the chance to compare this summer with last summer.

## Survey data is only useful if it reaches the guest profile

A survey answer becomes useful once it is attached to a known guest and a known stay. A 6 out of 10 with no name attached is just a number on a chart, and nobody can follow it up.

<ImageBlock id="guest-profile" />

Take a regular guest who rates breakfast poorly on her third stay. If the answer sits in her profile, reception sees the comment before her next arrival, and the pre-arrival message can mention that the buffet has changed. If it doesn't, the comment stays a line in a spreadsheet that nobody reads in March.

Store more than the score:

- the score and the free comment
- topic tags (breakfast, noise, cleanliness, staff)
- stay dates and room type
- the booking channel

These fields let you filter later, and filtering matters because averages hide problems. Couples, business travellers and families rate differently. A global score across all of them can look healthy for months while one segment slowly gets worse.

This is the main reason a CRM with built-in surveys tends to outlast a standalone tool: the answer becomes part of what the hotel knows about the guest instead of staying in its own silo. ## Never filter who gets the review invitation

Every guest must receive the invitation to leave a public review, whatever score they gave in the survey. Sending the Google review link only to guests who scored high is called review gating. It breaks Google's rules and can lead to removed reviews or a penalised listing.

<ImageBlock id="manager-callback" />

The correct setup has two parts:

1. Every guest receives the invitation to leave a review.
2. A low score also alerts the manager, who calls the guest back.

The alert is added on top. It never replaces the invitation. An unhappy guest still gets the review link, and also a phone call from someone who can put things right. In practice, that call often does more for the public review than any filter would.

<Callout type="warning">
Test this in every demo. Ask the vendor: "What happens to a guest who scores 3 out of 10?" If the answer is "they don't see the Google link", walk away.
</Callout>

Keep the survey and the review invitation as two distinct steps in the flow. The survey collects private, detailed feedback, and the invitation asks for a public opinion. Gating usually starts when the two get merged into one conditional screen.

## Setting up your first automated survey in a week

A first automated survey can be live within a week, provided the questions, the channel and the alert owner are decided before you start. Here is a realistic schedule.

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| Day 1 | Connect the PMS |
| Day 2 | Pick 5 to 8 questions from a template |
| Day 3 | Set the channel and the send delay |
| Day 4 | Set the low-score alert and who receives it |
| Day 5 | Test on your own booking |
| Days 6 and 7 | Go live |

Keep the survey short: 5 to 8 questions plus one open comment, so a guest can finish in about two minutes on a phone. The <InternalLink href="/en/blog/experience-client/hotel-guest-satisfaction-survey-questions">survey questions guide</InternalLink> has examples you can copy.

Name the person who receives low-score alerts before launch, and agree on a response time. Same day is a good target. If an alert has no owner, nobody reads it, and the team soon stops trusting the whole system.

<Callout type="tip">
Run the first two weeks as a pilot and read every comment yourself before trusting the dashboards. You will catch confusing questions and misrouted alerts early.
</Callout>

Response rates vary a lot by channel and by property, so don't judge a tool on a figure quoted by another hotel. Judge it on two things: does the survey leave every day, and are the alerts handled?

## Which setup fits which hotel

The right setup depends on the size of the property and on how much of the follow-up you want automated. A B&B and a small group do not need the same thing.

| Profile | Prioritise | Mistake to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Small B&B (under 15 rooms) | Price transparency, simple templates. Test with the free plan. | Paying for analytics you will not read |
| Independent hotel (20 to 60 rooms) | PMS sync, send timing, low-score alerts, multilingual surveys | Choosing a tool that needs CSV imports |
| Small group (2 to 5 properties) | Consistent surveys across sites, per-hotel reporting, exportability | Letting each property build its own questionnaire |

**What is the best hotel guest satisfaction survey software?**

The best one is the one connected to your PMS, so the survey goes out automatically after each stay and the answer lands in the guest profile. For independent hotels, that usually means a hotel CRM with built-in surveys rather than a standalone form tool.

**Is there free hotel guest survey software?**

Yes. Generic form builders are free, and TriggerFlow's Discovery plan costs 0 € per month. A free form works for a one-off test, but it will not trigger at check-out unless it is linked to your PMS.

**How much does guest survey software cost for a hotel?**

**When should a hotel send a satisfaction survey?**

Send it the day after check-out, with one reminder a few days later. The stay is still fresh and the guest has not yet moved on to the next trip.

**Can I send the Google review link only to happy guests?**

No. Choosing who receives the link based on their score goes against Google's rules. Send the invitation to every guest, and let a low score also alert the manager, who calls the guest back.

**Does survey software work with my PMS?**

It does if the vendor lists your PMS as a native integration. TriggerFlow connects to Mews, Thaïs, Opera Cloud, Medialog, Misterbooking, Asterio and Apaleo.
