## A hotel CRM is the guest memory your PMS does not keep

A hotel CRM is a guest database that also acts: it stores everything you know about each guest and uses it to write to them, sell to them and ask for their feedback at the right time. CRM stands for customer relationship management. In a hotel, read it as guest experience: what you say and offer before, during and after the stay.

Take a guest on their third visit. Right now, the front desk only knows if someone happens to remember. With a CRM, whoever is on shift sees the profile: three stays, the room they liked, the breakfast they ordered. The memory belongs to the hotel, not to one employee.

A common trap is treating a newsletter list as a CRM. A list holds email addresses. It can't separate families from business travellers, and it can't send a message three days before a given guest's arrival date. A CRM can do both because it knows who the guest is and when they arrive.

## What a hotel CRM stores about each guest

A hotel CRM stores one unified profile per guest: identity, stay history, preferences, consents, survey answers, reviews, conversations and extra purchases. Each piece of data comes from a different source.

| Type of data | Where it comes from | What it is used for |
|---|---|---|
| Identity and contact details | PMS | Addressing the guest, sending confirmations |
| Stay history | PMS | Spotting returning guests, segmenting |
| Preferences (room, diet, arrival time) | Forms and messages | Personalising the welcome |
| GDPR consents | Booking and pre-check-in forms | Knowing who you may write to for marketing |
| Survey answers | Post-stay survey | Fixing problems, tracking satisfaction |
| Reviews | Google, Booking.com and others | Following reputation per guest |
| Email, SMS and WhatsApp exchanges | Messaging channels | Seeing the whole conversation in one place |
| Extra purchases | Upsell offers | Knowing what each guest actually buys |

The profile has to be deduplicated. A traveller who booked once through an OTA and once directly should not end up as two records. If they do, their history and their consents are split between the two.

<Callout type="warning">
OTA relay email addresses are a classic trap. A guest who booked through a platform often has an anonymous address that expires or never reaches them. Collecting the real email at pre-check-in, for example with an [online form](/en/produit/formulaires), is what makes later messages arrive.
</Callout>

The data also has to be collected legitimately. Never scrape or make up contact details. Under GDPR, consent to marketing is separate from the booking, and guests can still ask to see or delete their data. A serious hotel CRM records each consent and makes deletion a one-click job.

## Where the PMS stops and the CRM starts

The PMS handles the reservation, the room plan and the invoice. The CRM handles your relationship with the guest around that reservation.

<ImageBlock id="pms-vs-crm-schema" />

| | PMS | CRM |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | Run daily operations | Build the guest relationship |
| Core data | Reservations, rooms, rates, invoices | Guest profile, consents, conversations, feedback |
| When it is used | At booking, arrival, departure | Before, during and after the stay, and between stays |
| Who uses it | Front desk, housekeeping, accounting | Manager, owner, marketing, front desk |
| Example actions | Bill a stay, assign a room | Send a pre-arrival message, win back a past guest, ask for a review |

A CRM does not replace your PMS. It connects to the PMS and reads your reservations. TriggerFlow connects to Mews, Thaïs, Opera Cloud, Medialog, Misterbooking, Asterio and Apaleo. The [Mews integration page](/en/integrations/pms/mews) shows how the sync works: reservations go straight into guest profiles, and nobody types them in again.

Some PMS include a marketing module. Most of the time it only does bulk sends, with no scenarios tied to where the guest is in their stay. That's fine for your annual announcement. It's not much use for a guest arriving on Friday.

## What a hotel CRM does before, during and after the stay

A hotel CRM sends the right message at each stage of the stay automatically, using the reservation dates in your PMS.

<ImageBlock id="guest-journey" />

| Stage | What the CRM triggers |
|---|---|
| Before arrival | Enriched confirmation, online pre-check-in form, offer of an upgrade or breakfast |
| During the stay | Welcome message, WhatsApp channel for guest requests, practical SMS on the day |
| After departure | Satisfaction survey, review invitation, offer to return |

**Choosing the channel.** Use SMS for anything urgent on arrival day, like a door code or a delay. Use email for longer content, such as a pre-arrival guide with photos. WhatsApp works best for back-and-forth, when a guest asks for something. TriggerFlow puts [email](/en/produit/email), [SMS](/en/produit/sms) and [WhatsApp](/en/produit/whatsapp) in the same guest profile.

**Upselling before arrival.** An upgrade or late check-out offered automatically a few days before arrival reaches guests while they're still planning their trip. See how [upselling works in TriggerFlow](/en/produit/ventes-additionnelles). As a reference point, the average basket across TriggerFlow hotels is <Stat id="avgBasket" />.

**Reviews.** Every guest gets the invitation to leave a review. Don't save the Google link for guests who seem happy: Google's rules forbid it. When a low rating comes in, the manager also gets an alert and calls the guest back. The TriggerFlow [review module](/en/produit/avis) works this way. The guide to [Google and Booking.com reviews](/en/blog/experience-client/google-booking-reviews-hotel) explains the rules in detail.

**Surveys.** A short post-stay survey tells you what went wrong before it turns into a public review. The [guest satisfaction survey guide](/en/blog/experience-client/hotel-guest-satisfaction-survey) covers when to send it and how to structure it.

**Direct returns.** The CRM remembers who came on business, who came with family and who came for a weekend, so you can write to each group differently and pay no OTA commission when they rebook. That's the idea behind [loyalty and win-back campaigns](/en/produit/fidelite).

## Do independent hotels really need a CRM?

Yes, once the same person answers emails, welcomes guests and handles reviews. That's exactly where tasks get forgotten, and forgotten tasks cost you direct bookings and reviews.

<ImageBlock id="owner-desk" />

Take a 20-room hotel with no marketing team. Three repetitive jobs take up the week: the pre-arrival message, the review requests and the follow-up with past guests. None of them is hard. All of them get skipped on a busy Saturday. Automating these three is where you get the time back.

## How to choose a hotel CRM without overpaying

Judge a hotel CRM on five points: a real PMS integration, a published price, no installation fee, the channels you need included, and GDPR compliance with hosting in Europe.

Ask every vendor these questions:

| Question | What a good answer looks like |
|---|---|
| Does it connect to my PMS? | Automatic reservation sync, not a CSV export you run by hand |
| Is the price published? | Yes, per month, on a public page |
| Is there an installation fee or a minimum room count? | No fee, no minimum |
| Which channels are included? | Email, SMS and WhatsApp in the same profile |
| Where is data hosted, and how is GDPR handled? | Europe, with consents recorded per guest |

## Getting a hotel CRM running in one week

You can get a hotel CRM running in one week: spread five steps over five days and start with a single automated scenario.

| Day | Step |
|---|---|
| Day 1 | Connect the PMS |
| Day 2 | Clean and import your contacts |

Start with one automated scenario, the enriched confirmation, and let it run for a while before adding others. If you switch on ten scenarios on day one, you won't be able to tell which one works and which one annoys guests.

Before any marketing send, check the legal basis: consent collected, an unsubscribe link in every message, and a record of both.

## Frequently asked questions

**What does CRM stand for in a hotel?**

CRM stands for customer relationship management. In a hotel, it means one profile per guest, used to send messages, offers and review requests throughout the stay.

**Is a hotel CRM the same as a PMS?**

No. A PMS runs reservations, rooms and billing, while a hotel CRM manages the relationship with the guest around the booking. The CRM reads data from the PMS instead of replacing it.

**Can a small independent hotel use a CRM?**

Yes. TriggerFlow has no minimum number of rooms and no installation fee, and the free Discovery plan lets a small hotel start without a budget.

**Which PMS can a hotel CRM connect to?**

TriggerFlow connects to Mews, Thaïs, Opera Cloud, Medialog, Misterbooking, Asterio and Apaleo. Whatever the vendor, check that the integration syncs reservations automatically and doesn't rely on manual exports.

**Is it allowed to ask only happy guests for a Google review?**

No. Google's rules forbid inviting only satisfied guests to leave a review. A good hotel CRM sends the invitation to every guest and also alerts the manager when a rating is low, so they can call the guest back.
