    The guest journey, one touchpoint at a time

    Between the moment a traveller discovers a property and the moment they come back, there are some thirty opportunities to speak to them. Most are never used, not out of indifference, but because no front desk team can handle them by hand.

    This document lists those moments one by one. For each of them: what triggers it, which channels it uses, what the guest experiences on one side, what the hotel does on the other, and what it actually changes. Everything described here is running in production today.

    How to read the cards. Every touchpoint carries its exact trigger, written as it appears in the tool. That is what separates automation from a bulk send: the message goes out because a precise event happened on that precise booking, not because someone launched a campaign on a Monday morning.

    Each touchpoint then reads in two columns: on the left, what the guest experiences, on the right, what the hotel does. In the summary view, each touchpoint fits on one line, one sentence per side. “Details” opens a touchpoint and its screenshots; “Expand all” opens the whole document.

    Guest side

    The visitor asks a question from the page they are reading, at any hour.

    The chat opens on the property's website, with a welcome message in their language. If they prefer WhatsApp, Messenger or Instagram, they write where they usually do. When nobody is available, they get a link to pick the conversation up later without typing anything again.

    Hotel side

    The widget installs with one line of code. Opening hours, translated welcome message, pre-chat form, offline message and colours are set without a developer. A visitor who writes on Instagram and comes back two days later on WhatsApp stays in the same conversation: no switching between five apps, no message nobody saw because it arrived on the wrong channel.

    What this changes: evening and weekend enquiries no longer sit unanswered until Monday morning.

    Website chatWhatsAppSMSEmail

    Guest side

    They get an instant answer, in their language, even at three in the morning.

    The bot knows the opening hours, the facilities, the cancellation terms, the address and the parking. As soon as a question falls outside its scope, or the visitor asks to speak to someone, a team member picks up the thread where it stopped. The visitor has nothing to repeat.

    Hotel side

    Phase 03

    The pre-stay

    The most profitable window of the journey, and the one most often left empty. The guest has already paid, they are picturing the trip, and they are available. This is where they accept an upgrade, fill in a form, book a table.

    Guest sideHotel side

    Online pre-check-in

    EmailSMSWhatsAppWeb form

    Guest side

    They complete the formalities on their phone, before they leave.

    Occupants' identity, estimated arrival time, preferences, special requests. Scanning their ID fills in the fields by itself, and they sign the house rules or the contract online. At the hotel, there is nothing left to fill in.

    Hotel side

    Before arrival, the front desk knows who has filled in the form, who is arriving late and what needs preparing.

    The form is built by drag and drop and translated into the property's languages. The scan reads the machine-readable zone of the passport or ID card. The day's arrivals board shows who has filled it in and who has not, and a code-protected occupants view can be shared with emergency services.

    What this changes: the time spent at the desk typing in identities disappears. This is also by far the phase where forms are best completed: close to a quarter of pre-stay sends are filled in, against under seven per cent after the stay.

    Hotel side

    The team's workstation

    The journey describes what the guest experiences. Here is what the team has in front of them to deliver it: four screens opened every day, connected to the same data as the messages.

    Dashboard

    Arrivals, in-house guests and orders

    The first screen of the day. It shows who is arriving, who is in-house, who is leaving and what needs preparing, in real time.

    Arrivals, in-house, departures

    Tabs for today's arrivals, in-house guests, today's and tomorrow's departures, with the room, dates, contact details and status of each booking. One click opens the details.

    Orders to prepare

    Upcoming orders appear in a calendar, and today's in a kanban: an order moves from one column to the next by drag and drop as it is prepared, then delivered.

    A screen of your own

    The dashboard is made of modules you arrange and resize yourself. You can create several, one per role: one for the front desk, one for management.

    Everything else, in one place

    Other modules cover forms, reviews, payments (collected today, deposits to capture), housekeeping, lost and found, maintenance and scenario results, over the chosen period.

    Upselling

    What the extras bring in

    Offering services is one thing, knowing which ones sell is another. Sales tracking brings together on a single screen what was ordered, through which channel, in which category, and what it actually weighs. It also covers sales made during the stay and those placed from the loyalty portal.

    Three headline figures

    Revenue, number of sales and average basket over the chosen period. The average basket is often the most telling: it says whether the guest is buying more expensively or simply more often.

    Compare two periods

    The period can be set to seven days, thirty days, three months, six months, a year or free dates. A second selection compares against the previous period, the same period last year, or a chosen interval.

    Compare two segments

    The comparison can also be between two guest segments rather than two periods. Couples and families do not buy the same things, and the catalogue can be adjusted accordingly.

    The split by category

    The weight of each family of services, in euros and as a percentage, on revenue or on number of sales. This is the view that reveals the categories taking up space in the catalogue without bringing anything in.

    The product ranking

    The ten best-selling services, sortable by volume or by revenue. A product that sells rarely but dear and one that sells often but cheap are not managed the same way.

    Where the orders come from

    Every order keeps its source: the pre-stay form, the loyalty programme or the Guest App. So you know which channel is working, not just how much you sold.

    Planning

    The room planning

    Every booking on a three-week timeline, room by room: what is occupied, what is freeing up and what is left to sell, at a glance.

    Three weeks at a glance

    The timeline covers twenty-one days, starting three days before today. Weekends and the current day stand out, and today's occupancy rate is shown at the top.

    Grouped the way you work

    Rooms can be grouped by type, floor or building, and each group collapses. The order can be set by hand when the logic on the ground does not follow the numbering.

    Statuses in colour

    Confirmed, pending, in progress, completed, cancelled: each booking carries the colour of its status. Cancellations are hidden by default, and filters are remembered per property.

    The booking in one click

    Hovering shows the guest's name; clicking opens the booking in a side panel, without leaving the planning. The screen remains usable on mobile.

    Messaging hub

    Every conversation, one screen

    Six channels, one thread per guest, and the whole stay in the conversation. The team replies with the full context, without opening three tools.

    One thread per guest, the whole stay inside

    Email, SMS, WhatsApp, website chat, Messenger and Instagram arrive in the same conversation, which also brings in the booking, payments, forms, orders and reviews. Each type can be shown or hidden with a filter.

    Working as a team

    Colour-coded folders, conversations closed and reopened, filters for unread, awaiting reply or assigned to me. A conversation can be assigned to one or more colleagues.

    Notes, quick replies and templates

    Notes only the team can see, pre-written replies by category and in several languages, email templates, and approved WhatsApp templates once the twenty-four-hour window has passed.

    AI assistant and translation

    Correct, generate a reply, rephrase or translate, in three tones and seven languages, drawing on the knowledge base and the guest's history. A whole conversation can be translated automatically.

    Bot, handover and delivery tracking

    A badge shows who is handling the conversation, the bot or a human, and the handover alert gives the reason and time. Every message carries its status, and failures are gathered in a log.

    Groups and mobile

    At group level, conversations from every hotel can be filtered by property. The staff mobile app carries the messaging, with notifications, quick replies and templates.

    Reviews

    Reviews and AI-assisted replies

    Reply to every review, quickly and in the right tone, without spending your evenings on it. The AI suggests, the team decides.

    Every review in one place

    Google, Booking, TripAdvisor, Airbnb and internal reviews, filterable by platform, rating, reply status and period. Internal reviews go through moderation before they are displayed.

    A reply drafted by the AI

    Two to four sentences that use the guest's first name, respond to what they wrote and draw on the property's last ten replies to keep its tone. Empathetic on a negative review, appreciative on a positive one.

    Always reviewed before sending

    The draft arrives in an editable field, and a free-text instruction can steer the wording. Nothing goes out without the team's approval.

    Published where the review was written

    The reply is published directly on Google. For the other platforms, it is copied in one click to paste on the site concerned.

    In batches when needed

    Up to fifty reviews handled at once: each drafted reply is reviewed, then published, copied or skipped.

    Alerts and summary

    Instant alert below a chosen rating threshold, and a digest daily, every twelve hours or weekly. An AI summary sums up what reviews are saying, and statistics track trends by platform and by property.

    Going further

    What happens on the floors

    Housekeeping is not a guest touchpoint. It is what makes several of them possible: a room ready at the time announced, a forgotten item found, a fault reported before the guest discovers it.

    The room board

    A kanban by status: to clean, in progress, inspected, ready. The status recalculates from the day's arrivals, departures and stayovers.

    Zones and buildings

    Rooms are organised into floors, wings and buildings, then into assignment zones. Manual sorting of the schedules is possible when the logic on the ground does not follow the numbering.

    Checklists

    Different inspection templates depending on the type of clean. A clean is only finished once the list is complete.

    Do not disturb

    Recorded and dated. The room is not counted as missed, and the guest is not disturbed twice.

    Lost and found

    Recorded with their location, annotated, and attached to the booking concerned so you know who to write to.

    Technical faults

    Reported from the room by category, annotated, followed through to resolution. Maintenance no longer runs through a notebook at the back of the office.

    The link with the journey. A room status moving to « ready » can trigger the message telling the guest they can go up. A service request sent by the guest from their phone arrives straight on the floors. The two worlds are not separate.

    Going further

    At group scale

    The question a group asks is not « does it work », but « does it hold across seventy properties without a dedicated team to maintain it ». That is a question of architecture, not of features.

    Shared templates

    A scenario written once at group level rolls out to the chosen properties. Changes propagate without rewriting hotel by hotel.

    One brand per property

    Each hotel keeps its logo, its colours, its fonts and its sending address. The same scenario produces a different message depending on which property sends it.

    A consolidated view

    A group portal aggregates the results of every property and lets you switch from one to another without logging out.

    Rights per property

    A general manager sees their hotel. A regional manager sees their scope. Head office sees everything. Data stays partitioned by property.

    A group guest database

    A guest known in one property is recognised in the others. Unsubscribes apply across the whole group, which protects compliance.

    A gradual rollout

    A library of ready-made scenarios, sorted by moment of the stay, lets you start a property without beginning from a blank page.
