    The guest journey, one touchpoint at a time

    Between the moment a traveller discovers a property and the moment they come back, there are some thirty opportunities to speak to them. Most are never used, not out of indifference, but because no front desk team can handle them by hand.

    This document lists those moments one by one. For each of them: what triggers it, which channels it uses, what the team sees on their side, and what it actually changes. Everything described here is running in production today.

    The journey, end to end

    A single unbroken line: twenty-six moments where a property can speak to its guest, spread across the three stages of the stay. Each step links to its detailed section.

    How to read the cards. Every touchpoint carries its exact trigger, written as it appears in the tool. That is what separates automation from a bulk send: the message goes out because a precise event happened on that precise booking, not because someone launched a campaign on a Monday morning.

    Phase 01

    Before the booking

    The visitor hesitates, compares, and leaves if nobody answers. Most questions asked at this stage arrive outside front desk hours.

    Visitor on a page of the website

    The website chat

    Website chatWhatsAppMessengerInstagram

    A widget goes onto the property's website in one line of code. Opening hours, translated welcome message, pre-chat form, offline message and colours are all configurable. When nobody is available, the visitor gets a link to pick the conversation back up later without retyping anything.

    Messages coming from WhatsApp, Messenger and Instagram land in the same thread as the chat. A visitor who writes on Instagram and comes back two days later on WhatsApp stays one single conversation.

    Team side

    Everything arrives in a single messaging hub. No switching between five apps, no message seen by nobody because it was on the wrong channel.

    What this changes: evening and weekend enquiries no longer sit unanswered until Monday morning.

    The AI bot and its handover to a human

    Website chatWhatsAppSMSEmail

    The bot answers in the visitor's language, detected automatically, from a knowledge base built from the property's website and completed by hand. It knows the opening hours, the facilities, the cancellation terms, the address, the parking.

    What matters is not that it answers, it is that it knows when to stop. As soon as a question falls outside its scope, or the visitor asks for a human, it hands over: the front desk gets an alert, picks the thread up exactly where it stopped, and the visitor has nothing to repeat.

    Team side

    Email alert and notification on handover. The full history of the exchange with the bot stays visible. A simulator lets you test the answers before going live.

    What this changes: continuous availability without an on-call rota, and a handover to a human before the visitor gets annoyed.

    Capturing the website audience

    Embedded widgetConfirmation email

    Two widgets slot into the property's website. The first collects newsletter sign-ups with double email confirmation, which protects deliverability and compliance. The second displays the property's real reviews, in a version search engines can read.

    A sign-up creates a record straight into the CRM, applies the chosen tags and feeds the segments. The contact is usable immediately, with no export and no import.

    Team side

    Contact record created, tags applied, segment fed. No data entry.

    What this changes: the website audience stops being anonymous and becomes a list you can address.
    Phase 02

    The booking

    This is when the guest's attention is at its highest. It is also the one message almost every property already sends, and the one they often send without having thought about it.

    New booking · Confirmation

    The booking confirmation

    EmailSMSWhatsApp

    The message goes out within seconds of the booking being recorded in the PMS, not at the next overnight batch. It carries the real details of the booking: dates, room, rate plan, amount, occupants, cancellation terms.

    The email editor is visual. The property's brand, its fonts and its logo are set once and apply to every message. A fallback link opens the message in a browser when the recipient's mail client truncates it.

    Team side

    The guest record is created or enriched automatically. A note can be posted back into the PMS so the front desk sees what was sent from the tool they already use.

    What this changes: a confirmation in the property's brand, not the PMS's or the agency's.
    Booking confirmed

    The card in the phone wallet

    Apple WalletGoogle WalletCalendar file

    The guest adds their booking to their phone wallet in one tap. The card carries the dates, the property name, the address, and the hotel's brand: logo, colours, image. It stays available offline, which matters when a flight lands.

    Alongside it, an add-to-calendar link drops the stay dates into the guest's diary, with the address and the property's contact details.

    Team side

    Nothing to do. The card is built from the booking data and the brand already configured.

    What this changes: the property stays on the phone's home screen right up to the day of arrival.

    Phase 03

    The pre-stay

    The most profitable window of the journey, and the one most often left empty. The guest has already paid, they are picturing the trip, and they are available. This is where they accept an upgrade, fill in a form, book a table.

    Arrival date, 3 days before, 10:00

    Online pre-check-in

    EmailSMSWhatsAppWeb form

    The guest fills in their details from their phone: identity of the occupants, estimated arrival time, preferences, special requests. The form is built by drag and drop, without a developer, and translates into the property's languages.

    Two things change the front desk's day. The ID document scan reads the machine-readable zone of a passport or ID card and fills the fields on its own. The electronic signature lets you have the house rules or the contract signed before arrival.

    Team side

    A table of the day's arrivals shows who has filled the form in, who has not, and who arrives late. An occupants view, protected by a code, can be shared with the emergency services.

    What this changes: the time spent at the desk typing in identities disappears. This is also by far the phase where forms are best completed: close to a quarter of pre-stay sends are filled in, against under seven per cent after the stay.

    Upselling

    EmailWhatsAppWeb catalogue

    Breakfast, transfer, treatment, bottle in the room, parking, late check-out: the catalogue appears inside the pre-stay form, with photos, prices and terms. Products can be conditional, for example only showing for stays of two nights or more, or only for certain room categories.

    The guest orders and pays online. The order reaches the property with its consumption date, ready to prepare.

    Team side

    A view of the orders to prepare, by day and by department. A tracking view can be shared with an outside supplier, the baker for instance, who ticks off what they delivered without access to anything else.

    What this changes: timing matters more than the offer. A proposal sent four to seven days before arrival converts markedly better than the same proposal sent the day before.
    Upgrade accepted

    The room upgrade

    EmailWhatsAppWeb catalogue

    The upgrade offer only goes out if the higher category is genuinely available in the PMS on those dates. The check happens at the moment of sending, not the day before.

    When the guest accepts and pays, the information flows back down into the PMS. The booking changes category without re-keying, and the acceptance itself becomes a trigger: you can follow up with a thank-you message or a particular room preparation.

    Team side

    The change shows in the PMS as if it had been keyed in at the desk. No double entry, no risk of overbooking the higher category.

    What this changes: extra revenue on rooms that would have gone at the lower category's rate.

    Practical information

    EmailSMSWhatsApp

    Address and directions, access by car and by public transport, parking, room availability time, direct contact for the front desk. This is the most read message of the journey and the least well used: it is the right place to slip in one last chance to book a table or a transfer.

    Team side

    Fewer calls the day before and on the day asking for the gate code or the breakfast times.

    What this changes: repetitive last-minute questions drop noticeably.
    Team side

    What the extras bring in

    Offering services is one thing, knowing which ones sell is another. Sales tracking brings together on a single screen what was ordered, through which channel, in which category, and what it actually weighs. It also covers sales made during the stay and those placed from the loyalty portal.

    Three headline figures

    Revenue, number of sales and average basket over the chosen period. The average basket is often the most telling: it says whether the guest is buying more expensively or simply more often.

    Compare two periods

    The period can be set to seven days, thirty days, three months, six months, a year or free dates. A second selection compares against the previous period, the same period last year, or a chosen interval.

    Compare two segments

    The comparison can also be between two guest segments rather than two periods. Couples and families do not buy the same things, and the catalogue can be adjusted accordingly.

    The split by category

    The weight of each family of services, in euros and as a percentage, on revenue or on number of sales. This is the view that reveals the categories taking up space in the catalogue without bringing anything in.

    The product ranking

    The ten best-selling services, sortable by volume or by revenue. A product that sells rarely but dear and one that sells often but cheap are not managed the same way.

    Where the orders come from

    Every order keeps its source: the pre-stay form, the loyalty programme or the Guest App. So you know which channel is working, not just how much you sold.

    Phase 04

    Arrival

    The moment the promise is tested. A late arrival, a crowded desk or a code that does not work leave a lasting mark.

    Arrival date, on the moment, 14:00

    Access codes and the smart lock

    EmailSMSWhatsAppSecure page

    The guest receives a personal link to a page showing their access codes and a QR code. Supported smart locks generate a code valid for the length of the stay, and only for that length.

    The page also carries a configurable contact block: who to call if the code fails, on what number, at what hours. That is the detail that avoids the panicked midnight call to a closed switchboard.

    Team side

    The code is generated and revoked automatically. No more keys handed over in person, no more keys never returned.

    What this changes: a self-service arrival possible at any hour, with no one required on site.

    Team side

    The evening rush smooths out. The front desk handles the cases that need a conversation rather than the queue.

    What this changes: a welcome capacity that no longer depends on how many people are behind the desk.

    Check-in on a room

    The welcome message

    SMSWhatsApp

    This message does not go out at a fixed time. It goes out when the check-in is actually recorded, so when the guest is physically in their room. The content follows: the wifi code, the breakfast times, the direct number to write to the front desk.

    Team side

    This message is also what opens the conversation channel for the whole stay. The guest replies on the same thread.

    What this changes: the guest knows who to write to, and writes instead of leaving unhappy without a word.
    Phase 05

    During the stay

    The shortest phase to describe and the most decisive. A problem solved during the stay never becomes a one-star review.

    Incoming message from the guest

    The conversation with the front desk

    WhatsAppSMSEmailChatMessengerInstagram

    Six incoming channels, one conversation thread, attached to the right booking and the right guest record. The team sees who is writing, from which room, until when, and what has already been said.

    The bot can answer simple questions at any hour and hand over as soon as it needs to. Service requests go straight to housekeeping: towels, an extra pillow, do not disturb.

    Team side

    A messaging hub on the desktop, and a mobile app for staff on the move, with notifications. A housekeeper can answer from the corridor.

    What this changes: the guest says during the stay what they would otherwise have written afterwards, in public.

    The Guest App

    Traveller mobile app

    An app for the guest, fed by the property's data: live stay information, room service and concierge, online check-in and check-out, direct chat with the front desk. The guest gets in through a personal link, with no account to create.

    Catalogues, products, stock, booking and loyalty data are synchronised continuously. What the guest orders in the app lands in the same place as everything else.

    Team side

    No database to keep twice. The catalogue set up once serves the pre-stay form, the guest portal and the app.

    What this changes: a digital room directory, replacing the leatherette folder on the desk.
    New order · Status change

    In-stay orders

    CatalogueConfirmationNotification to the point of sale

    An order placed during the stay follows its own cycle: confirmation to the guest, notification to the department concerned, status change when it is prepared, then delivered. A restaurant booking can be created automatically, and the point of sale notified.

    Team side

    Orders come up by department and by time slot. Extras already consumed and present in the PMS are reconciled to avoid counting the same revenue twice.

    What this changes: extra revenue becomes measurable and attributable to the message that triggered it.
    Phase 06

    Departure

    Often treated as an administrative formality. It is however the last impression, and the last moment when taking payment is simple.

    Departure date, on the moment

    Payment and the invoice

    EmailSMSPayment link

    The invoice is generated from the real data of the stay and sent. When a balance remains, a payment link comes with it: the guest pays from their phone, in the taxi, rather than at the desk while three people wait behind them.

    The detailed breakdown of services can be hidden to show only a total, when that is what the property wants.

    Team side

    Tracking of unpaid balances, automatic reminders, reconciliation at cash-up.

    What this changes: fewer unpaid bills to chase, and a desk free of queues in the morning.
    Check-out on a room

    Check-out and the forgotten item

    SMSEmail

    Check-out triggers both the message to the guest and the turnaround on the housekeeping side. When the housekeeper records an item found in the room, the information is attached to the booking that has just left: you know who to write to, and you write the same day.

    Team side

    The room moves to turnaround, the found item is recorded with its photo and location, and the case stays available.

    What this changes: a charger returned the same day is worth more than a perfectly written thank-you message.
    Phase 07

    After the stay

    Two distinct goals, not to be confused: understanding what happened, and getting a public review. Mixing them into one message degrades both.

    Departure date, 1 day after, 09:30

    The satisfaction survey

    EmailWhatsAppWeb form

    The form is built by the property: overall score, questions by theme, free text, sections that only appear if the previous answer warrants it. A negative answer on cleanliness can open a follow-up question nobody else will see.

    The sending time is not a detail. A message sent in the late morning is opened far more often than one sent in the middle of the night or at the end of the day.

    Team side

    Answers feed dashboards by theme and by period, and stay attached to the guest record. A funnel shows where respondents drop out of the form.

    What this changes: a measured signal on what works, rather than reading public reviews once a quarter.
    Form: answered

    The review request and online reputation

    EmailSMSWhatsApp

    The public review request is not sent to everyone. It is conditioned on the answer to the survey: a satisfied guest is pointed to the public review, an unhappy one is pointed to management. This is not a filter on the review, it is an order of priority on how each is handled.

    Google reviews are collected, matched to the guest record where possible, and can receive a public reply from within the tool. Reviews from Booking, TripAdvisor and Airbnb are also pulled in for reading.

    Team side

    Immediate alert on a negative review, periodic summary, reputation dashboards by property and by period.

    What this changes: a rising volume of reviews and a response time counted in hours.
    Email: opened · link clicked · no answer

    Follow-up conditioned on what the guest actually did

    EmailSMSWhatsApp

    This is what separates a scenario from a mass send. The journey continues differently depending on what the guest did: opened without clicking, clicked without answering, answered, or nothing at all. The follow-up message is neither the same, nor on the same channel.

    An email left unopened can switch to SMS or WhatsApp after two days. A guest who has answered never receives the follow-up.

    Team side

    The scenario is drawn visually, with its yes and no branches. Opens generated by corporate antivirus software are set aside so they do not distort the reading.

    What this changes: nobody receives the same request three times, and nobody is forgotten.

    Team side

    Updates made by the guest feed straight into the CRM. Tiers, points and benefits are configured without development.

    What this changes: a reason to come back directly rather than through an online travel agency.

    Set date · Recurring date

    Segmented campaigns

    EmailSMSWhatsApp

    Segments are built on real data: amount spent, number of stays, date of the last stay, country, room category booked, products already ordered, tags applied by the team. Some sixty criteria can be combined.

    A campaign can be one-off or recurring, and automatically exclude guests already enrolled elsewhere. Unsubscribes are handled at group level, which prevents a guest who opted out of one hotel from being contacted by another.

    Team side

    Results by campaign: opens, clicks, orders, attributed revenue. Sends that went through an online travel agency can be excluded from the statistics so they do not distort them.

    What this changes: you stop sending the same thing to everyone.

    EmailWhatsApp

    A guest who has not come back for eighteen months, who had spent above a certain amount and who has no future booking: the segment is described in three criteria and maintains itself. Guests enter and leave it over time, with no intervention.

    Team side

    The segment is alive. There is no frozen list to re-export every quarter.

    Cross-cutting

    What happens on the floors

    Housekeeping is not a guest touchpoint. It is what makes several of them possible: a room ready at the time announced, a forgotten item found, a fault reported before the guest discovers it.

    The room board

    A kanban by status: to clean, in progress, inspected, ready. The status recalculates from the day's arrivals, departures and stayovers.

    Zones and buildings

    Rooms are organised into floors, wings and buildings, then into assignment zones. Manual sorting of the schedules is possible when the logic on the ground does not follow the numbering.

    Checklists

    Different inspection templates depending on the type of clean. A clean is only finished once the list is complete.

    Do not disturb

    Recorded and dated. The room is not counted as missed, and the guest is not disturbed twice.

    Lost and found

    Recorded with their location, annotated, and attached to the booking concerned so you know who to write to.

    Technical faults

    Reported from the room by category, annotated, followed through to resolution. Maintenance no longer runs through a notebook at the back of the office.

    The link with the journey. A room status moving to « ready » can trigger the message telling the guest they can go up. A service request sent by the guest from their phone arrives straight on the floors. The two worlds are not separate.

    Cross-cutting

    At group scale

    The question a group asks is not « does it work », but « does it hold across seventy properties without a dedicated team to maintain it ». That is a question of architecture, not of features.

    Shared templates

    A scenario written once at group level rolls out to the chosen properties. Changes propagate without rewriting hotel by hotel.

    One brand per property

    Each hotel keeps its logo, its colours, its fonts and its sending address. The same scenario produces a different message depending on which property sends it.

    A consolidated view

    A group portal aggregates the results of every property and lets you switch from one to another without logging out.

    Rights per property

    A general manager sees their hotel. A regional manager sees their scope. Head office sees everything. Data stays partitioned by property.

    A group guest database

    A guest known in one property is recognised in the others. Unsubscribes apply across the whole group, which protects compliance.

    A gradual rollout

    A library of ready-made scenarios, sorted by moment of the stay, lets you start a property without beginning from a blank page.

    Mapping your own journey

    The table above describes a typical property. Yours has its own constraints: a particular PMS, late arrivals, business or leisure guests, a mixed estate. The useful work is to lay your real journey beside this one and look at the gaps.

    STEP 1

    Taking stock

    A one-hour session to list what is sent today, by whom, and when. The gaps show up on their own.

    STEP 2

    The technical review

    Checking what your PMS actually exposes, property by property, and which triggers follow from that.

    STEP 3

    A pilot property

    One hotel, three scenarios, six weeks. The pilot's figures form the basis for the decision on the rest of the estate.
